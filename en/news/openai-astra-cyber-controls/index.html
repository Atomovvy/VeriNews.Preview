In brief

On August 7, OpenAI said preliminary internal evaluations of its upcoming Astra model showed enough progress in cybersecurity that the company could not rule out the Critical level under its Preparedness Framework. OpenAI is also pausing internal Astra activities that do not yet meet strengthened security requirements.

This does not mean Astra has been independently proven to reach Critical capability, and it does not mean all development of the model has stopped.

Why this assessment

The main facts about OpenAI's decision are stated directly by the company and independently reported by Axios. The largest uncertainty concerns Astra's capability level itself: the public evidence currently consists mainly of OpenAI's preliminary assessment rather than an independent result set that would allow outsiders to reproduce a Critical verdict.

What is confirmed

  • OpenAI says its preliminary evaluations are strong enough that it cannot rule out Critical cyber capability.
  • The company is strengthening controls and pausing Astra activities that do not yet meet those requirements.
  • OpenAI says Astra was not the model involved in the Hugging Face incident.

What is partial, disputed, or contradicted

  • Public material does not independently establish that Astra actually reaches the Critical threshold. The supported claim is that OpenAI cannot rule it out based on preliminary evaluations.
  • “OpenAI stopped Astra development” would be too broad. The official statement refers specifically to pausing activities that do not meet strengthened controls.

What remains unknown

  • full independent Astra evaluation results,
  • Astra's final cyber capability classification,
  • the exact timing of any public release.
